## Overview

Swipe is a hand-management card game built from three combined decks — 168 cards total, four of them Swipe cards per deck. Everyone starts with 20 cards: four dealt face-down with a face-up card on top of each, and the rest in hand. The goal is simple to say and harder to pull off: get rid of every card in your combined hand before anyone else does. Whoever empties out first scores zero. Everybody else adds up the cards still sitting in their hand, and a leftover Swipe card is the worst thing you can be caught holding.

## Ryan's Review

### Likes
- Fairly simple to understand and very easy to play
- Strategy deepens the more you play — there's real skill in when you unload your high cards
- Some luck in the deal, but a good player can climb out of a bad hand
- Accessible to basically anybody, and a great game for camping trips

### Dislikes
- Shuffling three combined decks before every hand gets tedious
- Scoring can involve a fair bit of math if someone's left holding a big hand at the end of a round

## Getting It to the Table

We picked up the official version of Swipe, and honestly you don't need to. Three regular decks of cards with the jokers standing in as Swipe cards would do the job, as long as you've got enough jokers to go around. That's part of what sold me on it early — no board, no components to lose, just cards and a place to sit.

## Thoughts

**The core loop is a race, not a build.** Every turn you're either playing equal-or-lower cards onto the pile or getting rid of your worst cards fast. There's no engine to build and nothing to optimize across turns — just a constant question of what to unload now versus what to save for later. That keeps the pace quick, which is exactly what you want out of a card game that's meant to fill twenty minutes between other things.

**Holding a Swipe card is genuinely stressful.** Regular cards in your hand at the end of a round just cost you their face value. A leftover Swipe card costs you 20 points flat. Once you know that, every hand you're dealt with a Swipe card in it becomes a small piece of anxiety you're carrying around the table, and figuring out when to cash it in for a clean pile-clear versus holding it for later is one of the more interesting decisions in the game.

**The face-down cards add a layer you don't expect.** You can't touch those four blind cards until the face-up card sitting on top of them gets played, which means part of your hand is locked away and unknown even to you until the game decides to reveal it. It's a small twist, but it keeps you from ever having full information about your own position, which is a strange feeling in a hand-management game.

**Setup is the one real friction point.** Shuffling three full decks together before every single hand is more work than it sounds like, especially a few games deep into a night. The rules actually address this — you're allowed to just spread all the cards face-down across the table and have everyone grab twenty of their choice instead of dealing. We started doing that after the first couple of rounds and it made a real difference.

**End-game scoring can turn into actual arithmetic.** If someone's stuck holding eight or nine cards when the round ends, especially with a face card or two mixed in, tallying that up slows the momentum right when the round should be wrapping up. It's not a dealbreaker, just a spot where the game asks a little more bookkeeping than its otherwise light feel would suggest.

**It travels well.** No board, no dice, no fragile pieces — just cards that hold up fine getting tossed in a bag. We've taken it camping, and it's exactly the kind of game that survives a picnic table in the wind or a tailgate that's a little short on flat surfaces.

**Price and components are reasonable.** The official set runs under $20 and comes with three decks of durable cards, which is a fair price for something that'll get repeat use. But since the actual system is just three decks plus jokers as wild swipe cards, this isn't a game you need the official box for if you already own enough playing cards lying around the house.

## Conclusion

Swipe is a simple, quick-to-learn card game that rewards paying attention to what everyone else is holding, not just your own hand. It's a great fit for mixed groups, casual game nights, or anywhere you want something portable and low-commitment — camping trips especially. The three-deck shuffle is a minor annoyance, but the "grab twenty cards off the table" alternative fixes that easily enough that it shouldn't stop you from trying it.
