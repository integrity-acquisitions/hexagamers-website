[Pandemic](/pandemic-review/) came out in 2008, and Z-Man has not stopped building on it since. There are true expansions, standalone games that share the engine but not the box, three Legacy seasons, a dice version, a version where you play as the diseases, and a handful of licensed games running the same system under a Star Wars or Warcraft skin.

If you own base Pandemic and you go looking for "the next one," it gets confusing fast. Half the things with Pandemic on the cover don't need the base game. A few of them barely feel like Pandemic at all.

So this article is a map, not a ranking. We're going through everything with the Pandemic name on it, sorted by what kind of thing it actually is, with a note on what changes and what we've liked or not liked about each. We're deliberately not crowning a winner here. That's a [separate article](/best-pandemic-expansions/), because "which one is best" depends entirely on whether you want a campaign, a one-off, or something to bolt onto the game you already own.

One thing to get straight before the list: **an "expansion" needs the base game, a "standalone" does not.** Getting that backwards is the single most common Pandemic buying mistake, and it's an expensive one.

## The Base Game

### Pandemic (the base game)

[![Hexagamers](https://img.hexagamers.com/hexagamers-box-art/pandemic.webp)](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B00A2HD40E/?tag=hexagamers-20)

Everything below assumes you know this one, so here it is in a paragraph. Four diseases spread across a world map. Each player gets a role with a special ability, four actions a turn, and a shared goal of curing all four diseases before the outbreak track fills or the player deck runs dry.

It still holds up. Our full thoughts are in the [Pandemic review](/pandemic-review/), and there's a [simplified how-to-play guide](/pandemic-how-to-play-simplified/) if you're teaching it.

**Worth knowing:** base Pandemic caps at 4 players. If your group is regularly 5, that's your first reason to look at On the Brink, not a reason to skip Pandemic.

## True Expansions (you need base Pandemic)

These three bolt onto the base game. They do not work on their own.

### On the Brink

[![On the Brink](https://img.hexagamers.com/hexagamers-box-art/pandemic-on-the-brink.webp)](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B00CAG5LJ2/?tag=hexagamers-20)

The one to buy first, and honestly the one most groups should stop at.

On the Brink does three separate things. It adds a fifth player slot. It adds a pile of new roles and event cards, which matters more than it sounds. Role variety is most of what keeps Pandemic fresh across dozens of plays. And it adds three optional challenges you can layer on independently.

**The challenges are the real content.** Virulent Strain makes one disease nastier as the game goes on, with escalating penalty cards drawn each epidemic. Mutation adds a fifth purple disease with its own rules. Bio-Terrorist hands one player a hidden traitor role, moving secretly and infecting cities while everyone else tries to catch them.

The Bio-Terrorist mode is the divisive one. It turns a cooperative game into a hidden-movement chase, and it's slower and fiddlier than the rest of the box. Some groups love it. Ours has played it a handful of times and mostly goes back to Virulent Strain, which is the challenge that adds the most tension for the least rules overhead.

**What we like:** Virulent Strain is a genuinely great difficulty dial. The extra roles are worth the price on their own.
**What we don't:** Bio-Terrorist drags. The petri dish containers are cute but everyone ends up using the box insert anyway.

<a href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B00CAG5LJ2/?tag=hexagamers-20" class="check-price-btn" target="_blank" rel="nofollow noopener">Check Price on Amazon</a>

### In the Lab

[![In the Lab](https://img.hexagamers.com/hexagamers-box-art/pandemic-in-the-lab.webp)](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B00CE3IA44/?tag=hexagamers-20)

This is the expansion for people who have played a lot of Pandemic and want the cure step to stop being a formality.

Normally, curing is: collect five matching cards, stand in a research station, done. In the Lab replaces that with a separate lab board where you move samples through a sequencing, characterising, and testing process. Curing becomes a physical little sub-game with its own decisions and its own ways to fail.

It also adds an official solo mode and a team mode where two teams race on the same board, worth knowing about if you're mostly playing at two.

**Fair warning:** this one is the most demanding of the three. It requires both the base game and On the Brink to use everything in it, and the lab procedure adds real time to every session. First play with the lab board is slow while everyone learns the sequence.

**What we like:** it fixes the one genuinely repetitive part of Pandemic. The solo rules are a proper addition, not an afterthought.
**What we don't:** table space gets tight, and the added length pushes Pandemic out of its "quick weeknight co-op" slot.

<a href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B00CE3IA44/?tag=hexagamers-20" class="check-price-btn" target="_blank" rel="nofollow noopener">Check Price on Amazon</a>

### State of Emergency

[![State of Emergency](https://img.hexagamers.com/hexagamers-box-art/pandemic-state-of-emergency.webp)](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B00SDUQ3LE/?tag=hexagamers-20)

The third expansion, and the one that gets bought last for good reason, though it isn't bad.

Three more challenges, same modular approach as On the Brink. Hinterlands adds animal-borne disease spreading in from rural regions, with new board sections. Emergency Events shuffles nasty one-off events into the player deck, so drawing cards carries risk instead of being pure upside. Superbug adds a fifth disease that cannot be treated at all. You can only cure it, which flips your whole approach to board control.

It's compatible with the other expansions but doesn't require them, which is unusually accommodating.

**Superbug is the standout.** Not being able to treat a disease removes the safety valve Pandemic normally gives you. You stop firefighting and start racing, and it feels different from the base game in a way the other challenges don't quite manage.

**What we like:** Superbug. Emergency Events makes the player deck genuinely tense.
**What we don't:** Hinterlands adds board sprawl for a fairly modest payoff. By the time you own three expansions you're managing a lot of modules and it becomes a "what are we playing tonight" negotiation before you can start.

<a href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B00SDUQ3LE/?tag=hexagamers-20" class="check-price-btn" target="_blank" rel="nofollow noopener">Check Price on Amazon</a>

## The Legacy Series

Legacy games are campaigns. You play roughly 12–24 sessions with the same group, and the game changes permanently as you go: stickers on the board, writing on cards, tearing things up, opening sealed boxes. When the campaign ends, that copy is spent. That's the deal.

All three seasons are **standalone**. You don't need base Pandemic, and you don't need to play the seasons in order despite the numbering.

### Pandemic Legacy: Season 1

[![Pandemic Legacy: Season 1](https://img.hexagamers.com/hexagamers-box-art/pandemic-legacy-season-1.webp)](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B00TQ5SEAI/?tag=hexagamers-20)

The one that made legacy games a category, and still the most recommended entry point.

You play twelve in-game months. Each month gives you two attempts at its objective. Win the first and you move on, lose and you get a second try with some compensation. Characters gain scars and upgrades. Cities get permanently damaged. Sealed envelopes and boxes open based on what happens at the table, and the story escalates in ways we won't spoil.

Mechanically, Season 1 is the closest of the three to base Pandemic. If you know how to play Pandemic, you can start the campaign in ten minutes. That's a big part of why it works. The campaign layers on top of something already familiar, so the surprises land as story rather than as rules confusion.

We wrote play logs while going through it: [January](/pandemic-legacy-s1-logs-january/) and [February](/pandemic-legacy-s1-logs-february/). Both have spoiler warnings on them.

**The commitment is real.** You need the same group, ideally 2–4 consistent people, across about a dozen sessions. Campaigns die when one person can't make it for a month. Sort that out before you open the box.

Red and blue editions exist. They're identical apart from the cover art. Pick whichever is cheaper.

**What we like:** the moments the game changes the rules on you are genuinely unmatched. Losing a month stings in a way no other co-op manages.
**What we don't:** it's a one-shot purchase for the price of a full game. And the last couple of months lean harder on story than on interesting decisions.

<a href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B00TQ5SEAI/?tag=hexagamers-20" class="check-price-btn" target="_blank" rel="nofollow noopener">Check Price on Amazon</a>

### Pandemic Legacy: Season 2

[![Pandemic Legacy: Season 2](https://img.hexagamers.com/hexagamers-box-art/pandemic-legacy-season-2.webp)](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0716K46YP/?tag=hexagamers-20)

Set 71 years after Season 1, on a world that did not recover. It's the biggest mechanical departure of the three, and the most divisive.

The core inversion: you're not removing disease cubes anymore, you're **placing supply cubes**. Cities run out of supplies and collapse, so instead of firefighting infection you're running a logistics network out of a handful of havens. Cure discovery is replaced by exploration, and the map starts mostly blank and you reveal new regions as the campaign runs.

That blank map is the best part. Watching the board fill in over months, and knowing your group made those choices about where to expand, gives the campaign a shape Season 1 doesn't have.

It's also harder, and the early months are rough. Several groups bounce off Season 2 in the first few sessions because the supply economy is unforgiving before you've built any infrastructure. Push through that and it opens up.

**What we like:** the exploration and the map reveal. It's the most ambitious design in the family.
**What we don't:** the difficulty curve is front-loaded and punishing. If your group wants "Pandemic but a campaign," this is not that. It's a different game wearing the name.

<a href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0716K46YP/?tag=hexagamers-20" class="check-price-btn" target="_blank" rel="nofollow noopener">Check Price on Amazon</a>

### Pandemic Legacy: Season 0

[![Pandemic Legacy: Season 0](https://img.hexagamers.com/hexagamers-box-art/pandemic-legacy-season-0.webp)](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B08F73L5BM/?tag=hexagamers-20)

A Cold War prequel set in 1962. You're newly graduated medical students recruited by the CIA to shut down a Soviet bioweapon programme called Project MEDUSA.

Instead of disease cubes you're clearing Soviet agents off the board, and each character carries alias identities that let them work in different regions. There's a spycraft layer over the standard Pandemic engine: recruiting, moving covertly, building a dossier on the target.

In terms of how far it strays from base Pandemic, Season 0 sits between the other two. Further than Season 1, nowhere near as far as Season 2. If Season 2's supply inversion sounded like too much but you still want a campaign after Season 1, this is the middle option.

**What we like:** the spy theme is well executed and the alias system gives characters more identity than a role card usually does. Approachable if you already know Pandemic.
**What we don't:** the story doesn't hit the highs of Season 1, and some months feel like they're marking time before the next reveal.

<a href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B08F73L5BM/?tag=hexagamers-20" class="check-price-btn" target="_blank" rel="nofollow noopener">Check Price on Amazon</a>

## Standalone Reskins and Reworks

These use the Pandemic engine in a new setting. All of them play on their own, and none combine with base Pandemic.

### Pandemic: Iberia

[![Pandemic: Iberia](https://img.hexagamers.com/hexagamers-box-art/pandemic-iberia.webp)](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B09XZVFQNY/?tag=hexagamers-20)

1848, the Iberian Peninsula, four historical diseases: malaria, typhus, yellow fever, and cholera. No air travel, because it's 1848. You move by rail and boat, and building railways between cities is a core action rather than a background assumption.

The other addition is water purification. Cleaning water supplies in a region slows infection there permanently, which gives you a genuine choice between treating what's on the board now and doing preventive work that pays off later. Base Pandemic doesn't really offer that.

Also notable: there are no cures in the modern sense. You research treatments, which reduce spread rather than eliminating a disease outright, so eradication doesn't work the same way.

**What we like:** the railway building is the best single addition any standalone makes. The 19th-century constraint on movement makes geography matter far more than it does in the base game.
**What we don't:** it's harder than base Pandemic and less forgiving of a bad opening. Not the one to teach a new player on.

<a href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B09XZVFQNY/?tag=hexagamers-20" class="check-price-btn" target="_blank" rel="nofollow noopener">Check Price on Amazon</a>

### Pandemic: Rising Tide

[![Pandemic: Rising Tide](https://img.hexagamers.com/hexagamers-box-art/pandemic-rising-tide.webp)](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0778WSWL9/?tag=hexagamers-20)

The Netherlands, and the enemy is water. You're Dutch civil officials holding back floods, building dikes, draining regions, and installing pumping stations.

This one changes the geometry of the game. Dikes are built on the borders *between* regions rather than in cities, so you're managing edges rather than spaces. Flooding spreads through those borders, and a breach cascades in a way that feels meaner than a disease outbreak because you can see it coming several turns out and often can't stop it.

The hydraulic structures give you long-term construction projects to work toward alongside the immediate crisis management. It's the most strategic of the standalones.

**What we like:** the dike system is a smart reworking, and the theme is unusual enough to sell the game to people who are tired of disease. Seven roles, all distinct.
**What we don't:** no solo mode, which is a real gap given how many people play Pandemic alone. Setup is longer than base Pandemic and the board reads as busy until you're used to it.

<a href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0778WSWL9/?tag=hexagamers-20" class="check-price-btn" target="_blank" rel="nofollow noopener">Check Price on Amazon</a>

### Pandemic: Fall of Rome

[![Pandemic: Fall of Rome](https://img.hexagamers.com/hexagamers-box-art/pandemic-fall-of-rome.webp)](https://www.zmangames.com/game/pandemic-fall-of-rome/)

395 AD, and the diseases are barbarian tribes. Goths, Vandals, Huns, Anglo-Saxons and Franks march along historical invasion routes toward Rome.

This is the most changed of the standalone reskins, because it adds combat. You recruit legions, march them around, and fight invaders with dice. Losing a battle costs you legions. You can also forge alliances with tribes instead of fighting them, which is the equivalent of curing a disease and is usually the smarter play.

Dice in a Pandemic game is a genuine departure. The base game has zero randomness once cards are dealt, and here you can plan a defence perfectly and still lose the roll.

It supports 1–5 players with an official solo mode.

**What we like:** the invasion routes give the map real directionality; threats come from the edges toward the centre instead of popping up anywhere. Alliances over combat is a satisfying strategic tension.
**What we don't:** the dice will annoy Pandemic purists. Legion management adds a fiddly logistics layer some nights feel like admin.

Worth knowing before you go looking: Fall of Rome is out of print on Amazon, and searches for it now land on other Pandemic boxes. If you want a copy you're looking at [Z-Man's own page](https://www.zmangames.com/game/pandemic-fall-of-rome/) for stockists, or the second-hand market.

### Pandemic: Reign of Cthulhu

[![Pandemic: Reign of Cthulhu](https://img.hexagamers.com/hexagamers-box-art/pandemic-reign-of-cthulhu.webp)](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B01DN2BM9O/?tag=hexagamers-20)

Lovecraft country. Four towns instead of a world map, cultists instead of disease cubes, and shoggoths that spawn from unchecked cultist buildup and rampage through towns.

You're sealing four gates rather than curing four diseases, and the big addition is the sanity mechanic. Investigators go insane, gaining a permanent madness effect that changes how they play rather than just penalising them. Evil Stirs cards fire off nastier events than base Pandemic's epidemics.

It's tighter and shorter than base Pandemic. Forty minutes, four players max, smaller footprint.

**What we like:** the madness cards are the best thematic touch in any Pandemic reskin. Shoggoths moving toward you creates a visible, mounting threat.
**What we don't:** the smaller map means less strategic room, and it can feel swingy. A bad Evil Stirs sequence loses you the game with less warning than an epidemic does.

<a href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B01DN2BM9O/?tag=hexagamers-20" class="check-price-btn" target="_blank" rel="nofollow noopener">Check Price on Amazon</a>

### Pandemic: Hot Zone (North America and Europe)

[![Pandemic: Hot Zone – North America](https://img.hexagamers.com/hexagamers-box-art/pandemic-hot-zone.webp)](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B084ZZDSZY/?tag=hexagamers-20)

[![Pandemic: Hot Zone – Europe](https://img.hexagamers.com/hexagamers-box-art/pandemic-hot-zone-europe.webp)](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B094DR378C/?tag=hexagamers-20)

The pocket versions. Smaller map, three diseases instead of four, 30 minutes, and a much lower price.

North America came first, Europe followed. Europe adds Mutation challenge cards that apply special rules to one disease, and the two boxes are designed to be mixed, so you can combine components across them for a bigger game.

These are the ones to grab if base Pandemic never gets played because it's a 45-minute commitment and setup. Hot Zone is out of the box and running in a couple of minutes, which is a genuinely different use case.

**What we like:** the price, the footprint, and how well it works as a travel or lunch-break game. Nothing important was cut to get there.
**What we don't:** if you already own and regularly play base Pandemic, this is a lighter version of a game you have. Three diseases means less to juggle and correspondingly less tension in the endgame.

<a href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B084ZZDSZY/?tag=hexagamers-20" class="check-price-btn" target="_blank" rel="nofollow noopener">Check Price on North America Edition</a>

<a href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B094DR378C/?tag=hexagamers-20" class="check-price-btn" target="_blank" rel="nofollow noopener">Check Price on Europe Edition</a>

## The Odd Ones Out

Three games that share the branding but not really the game.

### Pandemic: The Cure

[![Pandemic: The Cure](https://img.hexagamers.com/hexagamers-box-art/pandemic-the-cure.webp)](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B00M4KGF5S/?tag=hexagamers-20)

Pandemic as a dice game. Six regions instead of 48 cities, and every turn you roll your character's personal dice to determine what actions you get. Infection comes from rolling dice into regions rather than drawing cards.

Setup is under a minute and a game runs 30 minutes. Seven roles, each with a distinct set of dice, and the push-your-luck element of re-rolling is the main thing base Pandemic doesn't have.

There's an expansion, **Experimental Meds** (ASIN B01L2VRZ8K), which adds a purple disease with its own dice and eight new roles.

**What we like:** speed. It captures the co-op tension of Pandemic in a third of the time and a quarter of the table space.
**What we don't:** dice mean the careful planning that defines Pandemic is largely gone. You're reacting to rolls, not building a plan.

<a href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B00M4KGF5S/?tag=hexagamers-20" class="check-price-btn" target="_blank" rel="nofollow noopener">Check Price on Amazon</a>

### Pandemic: Rapid Response

[![Pandemic: Rapid Response](https://img.hexagamers.com/hexagamers-box-art/pandemic-rapid-response.webp)](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07PKRNMLH/?tag=hexagamers-20)

Real-time, with a two-minute timer running. You're a crisis response unit flying a plane between cities delivering food, water, medicine and vaccines, rolling and re-rolling dice to manufacture what each city needs before its patience runs out.

There's no turn structure. Everyone acts at once, shouting, while the timer flips. It's a completely different feeling from the deliberate table-talk of base Pandemic.

**What we like:** it's a great 20-minute filler and it plays well with people who find regular Pandemic too slow or too talky.
**What we don't:** the dominant-player problem that base Pandemic has is much worse here, because there's no time to discuss anything. The loudest confident person just runs it. And "Pandemic" on the box sets an expectation this doesn't meet.

<a href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07PKRNMLH/?tag=hexagamers-20" class="check-price-btn" target="_blank" rel="nofollow noopener">Check Price on Amazon</a>

### Pandemic: Contagion

[![Pandemic: Contagion](https://img.hexagamers.com/hexagamers-box-art/pandemic-contagion.webp)](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B00M4KGFE4/?tag=hexagamers-20)

The joke version, and it's competitive. You play as the diseases, trying to infect and wipe out humanity faster than the other diseases. Card game, 30 minutes, no board in the usual sense.

It's the only Pandemic-branded product that isn't cooperative at all, which makes it a strange fit in the line.

**What we like:** the premise is funny and it lands well as a change of pace with people who know the original.
**What we don't:** it's a fairly ordinary card game once the novelty wears off, and it doesn't scratch the Pandemic itch even slightly.

<a href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B00M4KGFE4/?tag=hexagamers-20" class="check-price-btn" target="_blank" rel="nofollow noopener">Check Price on Amazon</a>

## Licensed Pandemic System Games

Since 2019, Z-Man has branded the engine "the Pandemic System" and licensed it out. These don't say Pandemic on the front, but they're the same designer lineage and the same core loop, generally with quests and dice combat layered on.

**World of Warcraft: Wrath of the Lich King** (2021) — Azeroth heroes against the Lich King's ghouls, with quests and dice combat. 1–5 players, 45–60 minutes. The most popular of the three. [Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B099NNRP9F/?tag=hexagamers-20)

**Star Wars: The Clone Wars** (2022) — Jedi against Separatist droid armies, same quest-and-combat structure. 1–5 players, 60 minutes. [Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0BG9XZKTG/?tag=hexagamers-20)

**The Lord of the Rings: Fate of the Fellowship** (2025) — the newest and the most mechanically involved. Each player controls two characters, protecting Frodo on the road to Mordor across 24 objectives and 13 playable characters. 1–5 players, 60–150 minutes. [Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0DYF9D44F/?tag=hexagamers-20)

If you like the Pandemic engine but the disease theme has worn thin at your table, these are where to look. Just know that "Pandemic System" means the skeleton is familiar, but the flesh on it is quite different, and all three run longer than base Pandemic.

## A Note on Editions

Two things that trip people up when shopping.

**Legacy colour editions are cosmetic.** Season 1 comes in red and blue, Season 2 in black and yellow. Matt Leacock has confirmed there's no gameplay difference, just cover art. Buy whichever is cheaper.

**The 10th Anniversary edition** is base Pandemic in a metal box with upgraded figurines and a higher price. Same game. It's a gift or a collector's item, not a different experience. Be careful searching for it, though: Amazon currently serves this edition for several older Pandemic listings, so check the box in the photo matches the one you meant to buy.

## Where This Leaves You

If you own base Pandemic and want more of the same game, On the Brink is the expansion that adds the most for the least friction. If you want a campaign, the Legacy series is the reason people still talk about Pandemic in 2026, and Season 1 is the standard entry point. If you want something that feels new rather than extended, the standalones are where to look, and Iberia and Rising Tide are the two that rework the engine most thoughtfully.

We're not picking an overall winner here on purpose.
