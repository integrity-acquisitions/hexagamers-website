## How a Turn Actually Runs

Four steps, every single turn:

<div class="steps">
  <div class="step"><span class="step-num">1</span><div><strong>Declare an action.</strong> Any general action, or any character action — no card required to claim it.</div></div>
  <div class="step"><span class="step-num">2</span><div><strong>Everyone else responds.</strong> They let it go, challenge the claim, or block it with a character of their own.</div></div>
  <div class="step"><span class="step-num">3</span><div><strong>Blocks can be challenged too.</strong> A block is just another claim — and just as likely to be a lie.</div></div>
  <div class="step"><span class="step-num">4</span><div><strong>Resolve.</strong> Unchallenged and unblocked, the action happens. Lose both cards and you're out.</div></div>
</div>

## The Bits People Get Wrong

**Income cannot be stopped.** Not blocked, not challenged. When you're down to one card and everyone is watching you, taking one coin is often the correct, boring play.

**A Coup always lands.** Seven coins, pick a player, they lose a card. No Contessa, no challenge, no discussion. This is why the coin count matters more than the bluffing — anyone sitting on seven coins is holding a guaranteed kill.

**Winning a challenge costs the challenger, not you.** If you're challenged and you genuinely have the card, you show it, the challenger loses a card, and then you shuffle your revealed card back and draw a fresh one. You lose nothing and gain information about who's willing to swing at you.

**Assassinating costs the 3 coins either way.** If the Contessa blocks it, the coins are still gone. Assassinate into a player you suspect is holding a Contessa and you've paid for nothing.

**Both Captain and Ambassador block stealing.** New players almost always miss the Ambassador half, which makes "Ambassador blocks the steal" one of the safest bluffs at the table.

## Which Bluffs Are Actually Safe

Some claims are far cheaper to make than others, because of how easy they are to challenge.

**Duke is the safest opening claim.** There are three Dukes in a 15-card deck and taking tax is unremarkable on turn one. Almost nobody challenges it early.

**Contessa is the safest defensive claim.** You only ever say it in response to an assassination, and the player who just spent 3 coins is usually not willing to spend a card to find out.

**Assassin is the most expensive bluff.** Get challenged and you lose a card *and* the 3 coins. Bluff the Assassin when you can afford to be wrong, not when you're desperate.

## Frequently Asked Questions

<div class="faq">
  <details class="faq-item">
    <summary>Can I claim a character I don't have?</summary>
    <p>Yes — that's the core of Coup. You can claim any character's action or block at any time, whether you hold that card or not. The risk is that someone challenges you and you lose a card for the lie.</p>
  </details>
  <details class="faq-item">
    <summary>Can a Coup be blocked or challenged?</summary>
    <p>No. Paying 7 coins to Coup always works. The target loses one card immediately with no counterplay, which is what makes coin totals worth watching closely.</p>
  </details>
  <details class="faq-item">
    <summary>What blocks stealing in Coup?</summary>
    <p>Both the Captain and the Ambassador block a steal. Many new players only remember the Captain, so claiming Ambassador to block is a reliable low-risk bluff.</p>
  </details>
  <details class="faq-item">
    <summary>Do I lose the 3 coins if my assassination is blocked?</summary>
    <p>Yes. The 3 coins are paid when you declare the assassination and you don't get them back if a Contessa blocks it. Only assassinate when you can afford to lose the coins.</p>
  </details>
  <details class="faq-item">
    <summary>When am I forced to Coup?</summary>
    <p>If you begin your turn holding 10 or more coins, you must launch a Coup that turn. You cannot take income, tax, or any other action instead.</p>
  </details>
  <details class="faq-item">
    <summary>What happens if I'm challenged and I do have the card?</summary>
    <p>You reveal it, the challenger loses a card, and you shuffle your revealed card back into the deck and draw a replacement. Your action then resolves as normal.</p>
  </details>
</div>
