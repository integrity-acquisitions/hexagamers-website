Draw a tile, place it to grow a shared medieval landscape, then drop one of your meeples to claim a road, city, monastery, or field. Score those features as they're completed and finish with the most points. Carcassonne is the game that introduced most of us to meeples, and it's about as easy to learn as a real strategy game gets. Give it five minutes and you're playing.

> **Note:** This is a condensed version of the rules to get you playing fast — nothing important is left out, but read the full rulebook for every edge case.

## What's In The Box

- 72 land tiles + 1 starting tile (darker back)
- 40 meeples (8 per player in 5 colours)
- A scoreboard + scoring marker per player

## Setup

<div class="steps">
  <div class="step"><span class="step-num">1</span><div>Place the single <strong>starting tile</strong> (darker back) face-up in the middle of the table.</div></div>
  <div class="step"><span class="step-num">2</span><div>Shuffle the remaining tiles into <strong>face-down stacks</strong> within reach of everyone.</div></div>
  <div class="step"><span class="step-num">3</span><div>Each player takes <strong>8 meeples</strong> of one colour. Put one meeple on space 0 of the scoreboard as your scoring marker; the other 7 are your supply.</div></div>
</div>

## How a Turn Works

Every turn is the same three steps, in order:

<div class="steps">
  <div class="step"><span class="step-num">1</span><div><strong>Draw and place a tile.</strong> Draw the top tile and place it adjacent to a tile already on the table. The edges must match: field to field, road to road, city to city. (If a tile truly can't be placed anywhere, discard it and draw another.)</div></div>
  <div class="step"><span class="step-num">2</span><div><strong>Place a meeple (optional).</strong> You may place <em>one</em> meeple from your supply onto a feature on the tile you just laid — but only if no one already claims that connected feature.</div></div>
  <div class="step"><span class="step-num">3</span><div><strong>Score completed features.</strong> If your placement completes a road, city, or monastery, score it and return any meeples on it to their owners.</div></div>
</div>

## What a Meeple Can Become

The meeple you place takes on a role depending on where it stands:

![A small stack of green Carcassonne meeples balanced on the table](https://img.hexagamers.com/Carcassonne-Meeple-Stacking_meramg.webp "The humble meeple — Carcassonne introduced most of us to them")

<div class="rule-cards">
  <div class="rule-card">
    <p class="rule-num">ROAD</p>
    <p class="rule-title">Thief</p>
    <p>1 point per tile in a completed road.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="rule-card">
    <p class="rule-num">CITY</p>
    <p class="rule-title">Knight</p>
    <p>2 points per tile + 2 per pennant (shield) in a completed city.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="rule-card">
    <p class="rule-num">MONASTERY</p>
    <p class="rule-title">Monk</p>
    <p>1 point per surrounding tile; 9 points when fully enclosed.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="rule-card">
    <p class="rule-num">FIELD</p>
    <p class="rule-title">Farmer</p>
    <p>Lies down. Scores only at game end: 3 points per completed city the field touches.</p>
  </div>
</div>

> **The catch:** you can never place a meeple on a feature someone already controls. But two players <em>can</em> share a feature if their separate claims later merge as the landscape grows — then whoever has the most meeples there scores it (a tie splits the full value to all leaders).

## Getting Your Meeples Back

You only have a handful of meeples, so this matters: a meeple returns to your supply **the moment its feature is completed and scored**. Farmers are the exception — they stay lying in the fields until the very end of the game. Spend all your meeples and you'll have turns where you just place a tile and pass.

## How to Win

The game ends when the **last tile is placed**. Then do final scoring:

- **Incomplete features** still score, but at a reduced rate (roads and cities: 1 point per tile; monasteries: 1 per surrounding tile including itself).
- **Farmers** score now: 3 points for each completed city their field borders.

Add it all up. **Most points wins.**

<div class="faq">
  <details class="faq-item">
    <summary>Can I place a meeple on a city or road someone already owns?</summary>
    <p>Not directly. You can only claim an unclaimed feature. But you can start a separate section and try to connect it to theirs later — if you end up with more meeples on the merged feature, you take the points.</p>
  </details>
  <details class="faq-item">
    <summary>How do farmers actually work?</summary>
    <p>Farmers are placed in fields (lying down) and never come back during the game. At the end, each farmer scores 3 points for every <em>completed</em> city touching that field. They're the most confusing part of Carcassonne — most groups get it after one game.</p>
  </details>
  <details class="faq-item">
    <summary>What if I draw a tile that won't fit anywhere?</summary>
    <p>Set it aside, draw a new one, and shuffle the unplayable tile back in. In practice this almost never happens.</p>
  </details>
  <details class="faq-item">
    <summary>Do unfinished cities and roads still count?</summary>
    <p>Yes, at game end — but at a lower rate (1 point per tile instead of 2 for cities). It's often worth claiming a big city even if you're not sure you'll close it.</p>
  </details>
</div>
