Both are cooperative. Both have you racing a clock while the board works against you. Both were designed by Matt Leacock, and if you've read our [Forbidden Island review](/forbidden-island-review/), you already know where we lean — Kaitlyn's dislikes list for Forbidden Island literally says "Pandemic does the formula better." That's not the whole story, though, and for a lot of tables the "better" game isn't the right first purchase.

We're not here to talk you out of either. We're here to help you figure out which one your group actually needs.

> Related reading: [Pandemic Review](/pandemic-review/) · [Forbidden Island Review](/forbidden-island-review/)

**Buy Forbidden Island if** this is your group's first cooperative game, you're shopping for kids or newer players, or you want something that fits in a bag. **Buy Pandemic if** you want the tension to actually feel dangerous, you're buying for people who already like strategy games, or you know you'll want expansions down the road.

## The Core Loop, Side by Side

Forbidden Island drops you on a sinking island chasing four treasures. Tiles flood, then sink, and you're racing a rising water level while trying to keep your escape route open. Pandemic drops you into a world with four diseases spreading across the map, and you're racing to cure all four before outbreaks chain-react into a loss you can't recover from.

They feel related because they are. Random role cards each game. A shared board everyone can see and argue over. A loss condition that creeps up on you turn by turn rather than arriving all at once. If you've played one, the other will feel instantly familiar.

**Where they split is stakes.** Forbidden Island's danger is contained — a flooded tile is a nuisance until it sinks, and each treasure has two possible pickup locations, so there's a built-in safety net. Pandemic doesn't give you that cushion. An outbreak in one city can chain into neighboring cities, and a bad epidemic draw two turns from winning can undo an entire game in seconds. We've had games of Pandemic go down to the literal last card in the deck. Forbidden Island rarely gets there.

## Difficulty Is the Real Decision

This is the one that matters most, so we'll say it plainly: **Forbidden Island is easier, and that's a feature, not a flaw.** The water level dial lets you set difficulty before the game starts, and even at its hardest setting it doesn't hit the nail-biting tension Pandemic reaches on a normal night. New players don't feel like they torched the game with one bad move, because the two-location treasure rule usually gives you a second chance.

Pandemic doesn't soften anything. Four action points, no do-overs, and the infection deck doesn't care that you're three cards from a cure. The "Pandemic Shuffling" mechanic — splitting the deck into stacks and seeding one epidemic into each before recombining — spreads danger evenly across the game instead of letting you get lucky with a quiet stretch. It's more satisfying to solve and more punishing to lose.

If your group includes someone who's never played a strategy game, start with Forbidden Island. If your group already eats worker-placement games for breakfast, Forbidden Island will feel like a warmup round, not the main event.

## Price and What You're Actually Buying

Forbidden Island is the cheaper box by a real margin, and the tin packaging is genuinely well made — compact, travels well, holds up. For what you're paying, the production value punches above its price.

Pandemic costs more, but it also has a much longer runway. There's a deep expansion and spinoff library (we've [ranked the best Pandemic versions here](/best-pandemic-expansions/)), so a Pandemic purchase can grow with your group for years. Forbidden Island has one direct sequel in Forbidden Desert, which raises the difficulty and complexity a notch — a natural next step once Forbidden Island stops being a challenge.

## Which One Should You Buy?

**Buy Forbidden Island if:** you're buying for kids, first-timers, or a group that wants a relaxed co-op night without real stakes. It's cheap, it's fast to teach, and losing doesn't sting.

**Buy Pandemic if:** you want a cooperative game with teeth — one that can genuinely go sideways and make the win feel earned. It's the better game for a group that already knows it likes strategy.

**Buy both if:** you can swing it. They're inexpensive enough as a pair that a lot of collections just have both — Forbidden Island for the night you want something light, Pandemic for the night you want to actually sweat. Start with Forbidden Island if you're not sure the table likes co-ops yet; graduate to Pandemic once they do.

Neither is a bad buy. The honest answer is that they're not really competing for the same slot on your shelf — one is the on-ramp, the other is where you end up once the on-ramp isn't enough anymore.
