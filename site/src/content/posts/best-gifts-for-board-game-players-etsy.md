I love getting new games. That part isn't complicated. But I was once given a game with a three hour play time, and with kids in the house that just wasn't doable in one sitting. It's a good game. We kept it to play later, and later took a while to arrive.

That's the problem with buying a board game for a board gamer. It's not that the gift is bad, it's that a game has requirements. It needs the right number of players, the right evening, the right amount of energy left at the end of the day. If any of those don't line up, a perfectly good box sits on the shelf waiting.

Honestly, we'd have rather been asked which game we wanted. Or been surprised with something board game related instead, which is a much easier target to hit.

So that's this list. Not games. The stuff that goes around them.

That's where Etsy earns its place. Almost everything below is made by someone who plays, solving a problem they hit at their own table. Dice rolling onto the floor, components sprawling everywhere, a fifteen-card hand that won't sit in one fist. And Etsy does the one thing Amazon simply cannot: it'll put somebody's name on it.

Everything here works for any board gamer rather than fans of one specific game, with two clearly flagged exceptions. There's a wide price range, from a few dollars up to proper centrepiece gifts.

Two notes before the list. If the person you're buying for is specifically a Catan obsessive, we have a whole separate list of [Catan gifts](/best-settlers-of-catan-gifts/). Nothing here is Catan-specific. And if they don't own many games yet, a gift from this list is the wrong call; start with [our list of the best board games](/best-board-games/) instead.

## Why Etsy Instead of Amazon

Three reasons, and one honest warning.

**Personalization.** This is the big one. A name, a date, a family name engraved on a game board. It turns a generic accessory into something specific to the person opening it, and it's the single clearest reason to shop here rather than anywhere else.

**The makers play.** Scroll enough of these listings and a pattern shows up: modular trays because one size never fits every game, magnetic dice towers because a permanent wooden tower is a pain to store, angled trays because scooping components back into the bag is genuinely the worst part of the night. Nobody designs that last one without having done it a hundred times.

**Small shops.** Your money goes to a person rather than a warehouse, which for a lot of people is reason enough on its own.

**The warning:** handmade means slower. Shipping takes longer than you expect, custom engraving adds days on top of that, and stock is thin. A listing that's live today can be gone next week. If this is a Christmas gift, order weeks earlier than feels necessary. Buying from a small shop is the reason it's a good gift and the reason it's slow, and you don't get one without the other.

## Inserts, Organizers and Component Trays

This is the category with the highest hit rate, because it targets the part of game night everyone quietly hates. Setup and teardown. A game that takes ten minutes to lay out and ten minutes to pack away gets played less often than one that doesn't, and organizers are how you close that gap.

One piece of buying advice before the list: **favour modular trays over game-specific inserts.** An insert cut for one particular box is beautiful and completely useless unless you know for certain they own that game. Modular trays work with whatever is on the shelf.

## A Few Buying Notes

- **Order earlier than you think you need to.** Handmade plus personalized can mean weeks. This is the mistake people make every December.
- **Check the shop's reviews and recent activity.** A listing with a long review history is far less likely to go quiet on you mid-order.
- **Read the "ships from" line.** A shop on the other side of the world can blow a deadline that a local one would make comfortably.
- **Triple-check the spelling on anything personalized.** There are no returns on custom work, and a misspelled name is a gift nobody can use.
- **Skip game-specific items unless you're certain.** Two items on this list are tied to particular games, the Magical Athlete insert and the Wingspan token upgrade, and both are only good gifts if you know that box is already on their shelf.

## Final Thoughts

The pattern across every good gift on this list is the same: it makes game night smoother without asking anyone to learn something new. A tray means dice stay on the table. Modular organizers mean the game hits the table more often, because setup stopped being a chore. A card holder means the person who's been quietly struggling with a wide hand all evening stops struggling.

If you want one recommendation, make it a set of modular trays or a dice tray. Both work with games they already own, neither requires you to know their collection, and both get used every single session. And if you know the collection well enough to get specific, the personalized pieces at the end are the ones people keep.

Buy the thing that goes around the games. The shelf is already full.
