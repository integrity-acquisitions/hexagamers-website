## Overview

You're a mobster divvying up stolen loot over eight rounds. Everyone's armed with a foam gun and a hand of cards — five "click" blanks and three "bang" bullets. Point guns, decide who to scare off, and stay alive long enough to walk away the richest.

## Whitney's Review

### Likes
- Fun and unique concept
- Strong theme that holds up throughout play
- Loot isn't just cash — throws in a strategy wrench
- Cute illustrations
- Expansion and character abilities available when you're ready

### Dislikes
- One player can get knocked out of the round very easily
- Not keen on the knocked-out-when-wounded mechanic

## First Impressions

I first heard about this game via Instagram — a photo of people pointing foam guns at each other, yelling about loot and greed. That, I thought, is perfect for our group. Cash 'n Guns is a light, quick game that puts everyone in the role of a mobster competing to come out richest — while staying alive.

We've only played twice, and we completely misplayed our second game (Wes ended up with all the loot both times — oops). Even with four players I could see how fun and chaotic this gets. More players is definitely better.

![Wes holding all the loot in Cash 'n Guns](https://img.hexagamers.com/Cash-n-Guns-Wes-Loot_h3xctt.webp "Wes with the loot in Cash 'n Guns")

## How a Round Works

Cash 'n Guns is quick and easy to learn. As a mobster, you have eight rounds to divide the loot from a robbery. Your goal: be richest at the end — but also stay alive.

Each round runs the same four beats:

1. **The loot comes out.** Cards equal to the number of players still standing get laid face-up in the middle. Everyone can see exactly what's being fought over.
2. **Everyone loads.** You secretly pick one card from your hand of eight — five "click" blanks and three "bang" bullets — and place it face-down under your gun.
3. **The Godfather counts to three.** On three, everyone points their foam gun at another player. This is the moment the game lives for.
4. **Chicken out or stay in.** Anyone pointed at can drop out, laying their gun down and forfeiting any loot this round. Everyone still standing reveals their card. Blanks mean nothing happened. Bullets mean a wound.

Survivors split the loot, taking one card each in turn. Three wounds and you're out of the game entirely.

The catch that makes it work: you only have three real bullets across eight rounds. Spend them early and you're bluffing with blanks for the rest of the night. Hold them too long and you never cash in.

## Thoughts

**The tension is real — and it all happens in seconds.** You're reading the room, deciding who's likely bluffing, deciding whether this round's loot is worth eating a potential bullet. If you get shot three times total, you're out of the game.

![The bullet and click cards each player chooses from in Cash 'n Guns](https://img.hexagamers.com/Cash-n-Guns-Bullet-Cards_fkkwzb.webp "Bullet cards in Cash 'n Guns")

**Loot isn't just cash.** There's art, diamonds, extra bullets, and health tokens. Diamonds and art stack for bonus points if you collect the most of a type — so the decision of which loot card to grab matters beyond just the face value.

![The different loot and card types in Cash 'n Guns](https://img.hexagamers.com/Cash-n-Guns-card-types-layout_nkcipd.webp "Cash 'n Guns card types")

The Godfather role adds a layer: that player can redirect one person's gun during the holdUp phase. Rotating who has that power keeps things interesting.

With only four players the game can swing quickly toward one person. More players means more targets, more bluffing, and more chaos — which is where Cash 'n Guns truly shines.

## How It Plays at Each Player Count

The box says 4–8, but those numbers are not equal.

**Four players** is the weakest configuration and the one we experienced first. With so few guns on the table, loot piles are small and a single player can run away with it — which is precisely what happened to us twice with Wes. Threats are easy to read because there are only three people who could be pointing at you. It works, but it's the version of the game people describe as "fine."

**Five to six players** is where it starts clicking. Enough guns that you can't track everyone, enough loot that backing down once isn't fatal.

**Seven to eight players** is the game as intended. The table gets loud, the pointing gets genuinely confusing, and the social reads matter more than the cards. Eight is chaotic in the way the box art promises.

If your group is usually four people, this is a game to borrow rather than buy. If you regularly host six or more, it earns its shelf space.

## The Wounded Player Problem

The thing I keep coming back to is the elimination. Get shot three times and you're done — and with eight rounds, that can happen well before the game ends. Sitting out the back half of a party game while everyone else yells and laughs is the exact opposite of what a party game should do.

It's not a dealbreaker, and it doesn't happen every game. But it's the one design choice I'd change. Some groups house-rule it so wounded players keep drawing loot at a penalty rather than leaving the table entirely, and that fix is worth considering if your group has someone who always draws fire.

## Which Version to Buy

The **second edition** is the one to get, and it's what's in print. The plastic foam guns are better, the art is cleaner, and it includes the character powers that add a lot of variety once your group knows the base rules.

Those characters are worth turning on early. Each gives a small asymmetric power — one player can survive an extra wound, another gets to shoot first — and they take a game that's mostly reading faces and give each person a slightly different angle. We'd skip them on a first play and add them on the second.

There's also **Cash 'n Guns: Live**, a real-time standalone version, and a handful of expansions. None are necessary. The base second edition with characters is the complete experience for most groups.

## FAQ

**How many people do you need to play Cash 'n Guns?** Officially 4–8. Realistically 6–8 is where it's good, and four is playable but flat.

**How long does a game take?** About 30 minutes for eight rounds. It's a filler or an opener, not a main event.

**Is it appropriate for kids?** The box says 10+. The guns are foam and the tone is cartoon gangster rather than anything graphic, but it is a game about pointing guns at people — some families will be fine with that and some won't. Worth a look before buying for a younger table.

**Do you need the expansions?** No. The second edition base game with the included character cards is complete.

## Conclusion

![A full table of players during a Cash 'n Guns holdup](https://img.hexagamers.com/Cash-n-Guns-Holdup_oxspzh.webp "A Cash 'n Guns holdup")

Cash 'n Guns is simple to learn, great for a wide range of ages, quick to play, and consistently entertaining. It's a party game where every person is their own team, and the foam guns make the whole thing feel ridiculous in the best possible way. Highly recommended for groups of 6+.

If your regular table is four people, borrow it before you buy — it's a different, thinner game at that count. If you host big nights, this is one of the easiest games to pull out and explain, and the foam guns do most of the selling for you.
