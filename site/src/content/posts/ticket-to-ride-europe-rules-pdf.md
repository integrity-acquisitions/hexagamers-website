## The Object of the Game

Score the most points. You earn them four ways:

- **Claiming routes** between two adjacent cities — longer routes are worth disproportionately more
- **Completing Destination Tickets** — a continuous path between the two cities named on the card
- **The longest continuous path** of routes, which wins the European Express bonus
- **Train Stations left unused** at the end of the game

Destination Tickets you fail to complete are subtracted from your score. That tension — take more tickets for more points, or play it safe — is the whole game.

## Tunnels, Ferries and Stations

These three are what separate Europe from the original Ticket to Ride, and they're where nearly every rules argument starts.

| Route Type | The Rule |
|---|---|
| **Tunnel** | Play your cards, then flip 3 from the draw pile. For each flipped card matching your colour, you must play **one more card of that colour** — or take everything back and forfeit the turn. |
| **Ferry** | Requires a specific number of **Locomotives**, shown on the route. Locomotives are mandatory here, not optional. |
| **Station** | Lets you use **one** route belonging to another player through that city, but only for completing your Destination Tickets. |

**On tunnels:** Locomotives count as a match for any colour, so they'll trigger the surcharge. If you claim a tunnel using only Locomotives, you only pay extra when a Locomotive is flipped. Always keep a spare card or two before attempting a long tunnel — getting bounced off one wastes an entire turn.

**On stations:** each station only ever borrows one route, no matter how many tickets that helps. And each one you *don't* build is worth 4 points at the end, so building all three is usually a mistake.

## Scoring

Points for claiming a route, scored the moment you place the trains:

| Route Length | Points |
|---|---|
| 1 | 1 |
| 2 | 2 |
| 3 | 4 |
| 4 | 7 |
| 6 | 15 |
| 8 | 21 |

At the end of the game:

- **Completed Destination Tickets** — add the value printed on the card
- **Incomplete Destination Tickets** — subtract the value printed on the card
- **European Express bonus** — +10 for the longest continuous path (stations don't count toward it)
- **Unused Train Stations** — +4 each

## End of the Game

When any player finishes their turn with **2 or fewer train cars remaining**, everyone (including that player) gets one final turn. Then reveal tickets and total the scores.

Ties go to whoever completed the most Destination Tickets. Still tied? The European Express bonus card breaks it.

## Frequently Asked Questions

<div class="faq">
  <details class="faq-item">
    <summary>Where can I download the official Ticket to Ride Europe rules PDF?</summary>
    <p>Days of Wonder, the publisher, hosts it free on their CDN. The standard 2019 edition rulebook is linked at the top of this page; there's a separate PDF for the 15th Anniversary Edition, which has different components.</p>
  </details>
  <details class="faq-item">
    <summary>How do tunnels work in Ticket to Ride Europe?</summary>
    <p>After you play your cards for a tunnel, flip the top 3 cards of the draw pile. Every flipped card matching the colour you played means one extra card of that colour. If you can't or won't pay, take your cards back — the turn is simply lost.</p>
  </details>
  <details class="faq-item">
    <summary>Do I need Locomotives for ferry routes?</summary>
    <p>Yes. Ferries show a Locomotive symbol on one or more spaces, and those must be paid with actual Locomotive cards. No other colour substitutes.</p>
  </details>
  <details class="faq-item">
    <summary>What are train stations for?</summary>
    <p>A station lets you use one route owned by another player passing through that city, purely to complete your own Destination Tickets. Each unused station is worth 4 points at the end, so build them only when a ticket is genuinely blocked.</p>
  </details>
  <details class="faq-item">
    <summary>How many cards do you start with in Ticket to Ride Europe?</summary>
    <p>Four Train Car cards and four Destination Tickets (1 long, 3 short), of which you must keep at least two.</p>
  </details>
</div>
