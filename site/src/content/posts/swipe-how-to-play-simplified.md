Swipe is a race to empty your hand before everyone else. You'll play cards equal to or lower than the last player, clear the pile with a 4-of-a-kind or a SWIPE card, and try not to get caught holding cards — especially a SWIPE card — when someone else goes out. Here's the whole game in about 5 minutes.

> Full thoughts: [Swipe Review](/swipe-review/)

> **Note:** This is a condensed version of the rules to get you playing fast — nothing important is left out, but read the full rulebook for every edge case.

## What's In The Box

- 3 SWIPE decks, 56 cards each (168 cards total) — a standard 52-card deck plus 4 SWIPE cards per deck, one per suit
- An official score sheet (optional — any paper works)

## Setup

<div class="steps">
  <div class="step"><span class="step-num">1</span><div>Combine all 3 decks and shuffle. The official method: spread all the cards face-down on the table, mix them around, push them back into a pile, and repeat a few times. It's faster than a full riffle shuffle with 168 cards.</div></div>
  <div class="step"><span class="step-num">2</span><div>Each player deals themselves <strong>20 cards</strong> from the shuffled pile, face-down, without looking at them.</div></div>
  <div class="step"><span class="step-num">3</span><div>Without looking at the faces, each player places <strong>4 cards face-down</strong> side by side in front of themselves, then puts <strong>1 face-up card</strong> on top of each — this is your table row (8 cards total).</div></div>
  <div class="step"><span class="step-num">4</span><div>Count your remaining cards to confirm you have <strong>12 left in hand</strong>. Your combined hand for the round is 20 cards: 4 face-down + 4 face-up + 12 in hand.</div></div>
  <div class="step"><span class="step-num">5</span><div>To set the first-round lead, each player draws a "test" card from the leftover deck. Highest card leads. The lead then rotates one player each new hand.</div></div>
</div>

## How a Turn Works

The leader starts an empty pile by playing 1 to 4 cards of equal value, or a SWIPE card. After that, on your turn you can do one of two things:

<div class="rule-cards">
  <div class="rule-card">
    <p class="rule-num">Option 1</p>
    <p class="rule-title">Play Equal or Lower</p>
    <p>Play 1 to 4 cards from your hand, table row, or (once unlocked) face-down cards, of a rank <strong>equal to or lower</strong> than the last cards played. If you're matching the pile's current rank, the total copies of that rank in the pile (yours plus what's already there) can't exceed 4. Declare your count out loud — "3 nines" — to end your turn.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="rule-card">
    <p class="rule-num">Option 2</p>
    <p class="rule-title">Play a SWIPE Card</p>
    <p>Play a SWIPE card at any time, on any pile. Declare "SWIPE" — the entire pile, SWIPE card included, gets moved to the discard. You immediately go again and start a fresh, empty pile.</p>
  </div>
</div>

**Playing higher instead:** if you can't (or don't want to) play equal or lower, you may play 1 to 4 cards of a *higher* rank. Declare the total count of that rank now in the pile — but this time, everything else already in the pile gets picked up into your hand, and only your higher card(s) stay on the table to start the next pile.

**Your table row cards play directly onto the pile** — never move them to your hand first. You can add more cards of the same rank from your hand afterward, before you declare your final count and end your turn.

**Face-down cards are locked** until the face-up card sitting on top of them has been played. Once that happens, the card underneath joins your playable hand — but you play it blind, since you don't know its value until you commit to it.

## 4 of a Kind

If your play brings a rank's count in the pile to exactly 4 (yours plus what's already there), declare "4 of a kind." What happens next depends on whether that rank beat the previous play:

- **Equal or lower than the previous play:** the whole pile (all 4 of that rank, plus anything else there) moves to the discard. You go again with a new pile.
- **Higher than the previous play:** only the 4-of-a-kind goes to the discard. Every other card in the pile gets picked up into your hand. You still go again with a new pile.

Either way, hitting 4 of a kind means an extra turn — same as swiping.

## Winning and Scoring

The round ends the instant any player plays their last card. Play stops immediately — that player scores **0** for the round. Everyone else counts up the cards still in their combined hand (table row plus hand) as penalty points:

<div class="rule-cards">
  <div class="rule-card">
    <p class="rule-num">20 pts</p>
    <p class="rule-title">SWIPE Card</p>
    <p>Any SWIPE card left in your hand at round end costs 20 points — by far the worst card to get caught holding.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="rule-card">
    <p class="rule-num">10 pts</p>
    <p class="rule-title">10 through King</p>
    <p>Every 10, Jack, Queen, or King left in hand counts as a flat 10 points, regardless of exact rank.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="rule-card">
    <p class="rule-num">Face Value</p>
    <p class="rule-title">2 through 9</p>
    <p>Number cards count their printed value. An Ace counts as just 1 point.</p>
  </div>
</div>

Lower total score is better — you're trying to avoid points, not collect them. Play multiple hands, rotating the lead each time, and total the scores across hands. Before you start, agree on an end condition: a set number of times each player has led, or a target penalty total (100–200 points is typical) that ends the game once someone crosses it. Lowest total score wins.

## Frequently Asked Questions

<div class="faq">
  <details class="faq-item">
    <summary>Can I play a SWIPE card even if I could legally play equal or lower instead?</summary>
    <p>Yes. A SWIPE card can be played at any time on your turn, regardless of what's already in the pile or what other legal plays you have available.</p>
  </details>
  <details class="faq-item">
    <summary>What happens if I can't play equal, lower, or a SWIPE card?</summary>
    <p>You pick up the pile. If you have no legal play — including from your locked face-down cards, which stay locked either way — you take every card in the central pile into your hand, and it becomes part of your combined hand for the rest of the round.</p>
  </details>
  <details class="faq-item">
    <summary>Do the face-down cards ever get shuffled or replaced?</summary>
    <p>No. The four face-down cards you set at the start of the round stay exactly where they are until their covering face-up card is played. There's no reshuffling mid-round.</p>
  </details>
  <details class="faq-item">
    <summary>How many SWIPE cards are in the game?</summary>
    <p>12 total — each of the 3 decks contains 4 SWIPE cards, one per suit.</p>
  </details>
</div>
